There’s Peace Behind the Rock: Peter Obi’s Visit to Asumpta Cathedral Signals a New Dawn for Imo.

The ancient city of Owerri witnessed a profound convergence of faith, leadership, and destiny over the weekend, as Mr. Peter Obi, presidential candidate of the Nigeria Democratic Congress (NDC), graced the Maria Assumpta Cathedral for a landmark event hosted by the Catholic Men Organization (CMO). The occasion, which saw the conferment of a Lifetime Achievement Award on Chief Martin Agbaso (Ochoudo), was far more than a ceremonial gathering. It was a symbolic homecoming a quiet but unmistakable declaration that a New Imo is on the horizon.

The Maria Assumpta Cathedral, fondly called The Rock by Owerri faithful, has long served as a spiritual and civic anchor for the Archdiocese. The CMO, under the leadership of its parish president Dr. Alphonsus U.C. Ukozor, has consistently used its platform to honour men of integrity and to convene conversations that elevate the moral and developmental trajectory of the state. The decision to honour Chief Agbaso a man whose life has been a testament to courage, philanthropy, and unyielding service placed the event squarely within that noble tradition.

Chief Martin Agbaso, born April 5, 1959, in Emekuku, Owerri North Local Government Area, is a Nigerian economist, accomplished entrepreneur, and former Senator for Imo East. Known across Igboland and beyond as Ochoudo, "the Peacemaker" Agbaso has built a reputation that transcends partisan politics. His academic laurels include being the first African elected Secretary General of the International Students Committee at the Pontifical University in Rome, and the first African recipient of the Clyde Winfield Academic Award for Excellence at the State University of New York.

In the corporate world, Agbaso rose from Management Trainee to Financial Analyst at Prudential Inc., New York, before founding Agad Nigeria Limited and building Nathan Limited into Africa's largest garment processing company. His Agbaso Integrated Farms, an advanced technology-driven agricultural enterprise, reflects his lifelong conviction that Imo and Nigeria can produce, export, and employ at scale.

Yet it is his political journey that lends the Ochoudo name its deepest resonance. In 2007, Agbaso won what many still regard as one of the freest governorship elections ever conducted in Imo State, a mandate that was subsequently annulled by INEC after the cancellation of results in nine Local Government Areas. Rather than descend into bitterness, Agbaso transformed that setback into a period of preparation and deepened engagement with Ndi Imo. Today, he has formally declared his intention to contest the 2027 Imo governorship election under the NDC, declaring: “The mandate was delayed, not denied.” His campaign platform prioritises healthcare, agriculture, technology, business growth, and investment with a pledge to deliver measurable improvements within his first year.

Mr. Peter Obi’s attendance at the event was neither accidental nor merely social. It was deeply symbolic. Peter Obi and Martin Agbaso share far more than a political party affiliation; they share a philosophy of leadership rooted in fiscal discipline, moral integrity, and sacrificial service. Agbaso served as Chairman of the Peter Obi Presidential Campaign Council in Imo State during the 2023 election cycle, and his confidence in Obi’s capacity to save Nigeria has never wavered.

Peter Obi’s antecedents speak for themselves. As Governor of Anambra State from 2006 to 2014, he left approximately $150 million in the state treasury a feat no successor has matched, and established the first sub-national sovereign wealth fund in Nigeria. He used the same Peugeot 404 official vehicle throughout his first term, flew economy class, and maintained a modest lifestyle without domestic staff, personally serving his guests at home. In 2024 alone, he committed over ₦1.5 billion to humanitarian causes, directing resources toward hospitals and nursing colleges rather than luxury vehicles. He is, in every sense, the antithesis of the profligate politics that has stunted Imo and Nigeria.

Obi’s visit to Asumpta Cathedral is, therefore, a powerful endorsement of the Ochoudo vision, a vision that mirrors Obi’s own: leadership that is frugal, accountable, and relentlessly people-centred. It signals that the movement for a New Imo is not a solitary ambition but part of a broader awakening across the Southeast and Nigeria.

The 2027 Imo governorship election, expected in November, presents a historic opportunity for Ndi Imo to choose a different path. The challenges are well documented: insecurity, unemployment, decayed infrastructure, and a pervasive sense of institutional abandonment.

Martin Agbaso is that kind of man. And Peter Obi’s presence at The Rock is a declaration that the search for such men is no longer confined to prayer halls and church pews. It has entered the public square.

The message from Asumpta Cathedral is clear: There is peace behind the Rock. The Peacemaker has answered the call. The New Imo has begun.
